Production

A self-learning PII/PHI column classifier

Finds the sensitive columns in our pipelines, including every new column that arrives.

Pattern matching alone could not do this, because many harmless columns look like sensitive ones.

  • Labeled sample tables with an LLM and used them as training data. New columns feed back into the loop.
  • Added spaCy keyword matching with scores on top of regex, so column context decides the label where value patterns are ambiguous.
  • Evaluated against columns that data analysts had labeled by hand.

Role designed, built and shipped

A Confluence chatbot that answers only from the docs

Ask a question and get an answer grounded in our own documentation.

A retrieval-augmented chatbot (RAG) built over our Confluence documentation. It is designed to answer only what the docs say, and not to guess.

  • Turned the Confluence pages into vector embeddings.
  • For each question, fetched the most similar passages and answered from them.
  • Read images in the docs with Azure's image-reading AI, so their content could be used in answers too.

Approach

How I build

Three habits that run through my work.

Design for two speeds

Some questions need an answer now and some need a careful one. The claims system serves both, in under 400 ms and in under an hour.

Measure against real labels

My PII/PHI classifier was scored against columns analysts had labeled by hand, and regex alone was the baseline it had to beat.

Keep AI grounded in data

My incident agent queries the real tables before it answers, and my Confluence chatbot answers only from the docs.
